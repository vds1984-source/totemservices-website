How to Plan a Digital Marketing Budget in Kurukshetra

Compare marketing scopes with clear objectives, defined deliverables and realistic measurement rather than choosing a package by post count alone.

By Totem Management & Consultancy · 3 October 2026

Choose the Outcome Before the Package

A marketing budget should start with the business problem. A school may need relevant admission enquiries; a manufacturer may need quotation requests; a retailer may need purchases and repeat customers. “More posts” is an activity, not a complete business objective.

Write down the service or product you want to grow, the customer you want to reach, the area you can serve and the outcome you can record. That gives the agency a basis for recommending channels and deliverables.

Separate the Main Cost Components

Agency Scope

Strategy, account management, content planning, publishing, campaign management, SEO and reporting should be defined in the proposal. Confirm what is included and who is responsible for delivery.

Advertising Spend

Google and Meta media budgets pay the advertising platforms. Keep them separate from management fees so spend and campaign performance can be reviewed clearly.

Production and Technology

Shoots, editing, design, website work, hosting, software and integrations can create separate costs. Identify them before agreeing the monthly scope.

Match the Work to the Customer Journey

Customers Already Searching

Review your website and Google Business Profile, the services customers search for and the path to contact. Consider SEO and local SEO alongside relevant paid search where the scope supports it.

Customers Need to Understand You

Use useful explanations, product or service demonstrations and credible creative work. Social content and video production need a clear audience and purpose.

Enquiries Are Being Lost

Review contact forms, response time, lead records and follow-up before simply increasing traffic. CRM and lead management can help organise the hand-off.

Use These Questions to Compare Proposals

  1. What business objective will the proposed work support?
  2. Which deliverables are included, and how often will they be provided?
  3. Are advertising spend, production, taxes and software costs identified clearly?
  4. Who owns the accounts, website assets and reporting access?
  5. How will content and campaign approvals work?
  6. Which metrics will be reviewed, and how will qualified enquiries be distinguished from clicks?
  7. Who will implement website changes and handle incoming leads?
  8. What will the monthly review decide for the next period?

The cheapest proposal may cover a different scope. Compare responsibilities and deliverables before comparing the total.

Look for Evidence With Its Limits Explained

A useful case study names the business, reporting period, channels and measurement limits. Totem’s Sobti Nursing Home and Virk Fertility pages provide published examples from local healthcare engagements.

Use examples to understand the approach and reporting. Views are not appointments, a call-button click is not a completed call, and another client’s result is not a forecast for your business. Your customer journey, assets and starting position affect the plan.

Review the Scope as the Business Learns

At the monthly review, compare the same period and agreed metrics, examine enquiry quality and listen to the team handling contacts. Decide which content, service pages, campaign settings or follow-up steps need attention next.

If the available records cannot yet connect marketing to sales, improve that measurement before making a precise return-on-investment claim. A report should make uncertainty visible rather than convert every interaction into revenue.
