Hospital enquiries versus appointments: what to count

By Totem Management & Consultancy · Prepared 16 October 2026

A hospital marketing report may show views, calls, enquiries and conversions. Each number can help you make a decision, provided you know what it measures. Reporting on hospital enquiries versus appointments starts with a clear record of what happened after someone expressed interest.

A click on a phone button does not establish that a call connected. A form event does not establish that your team received a usable enquiry. A saved enquiry still needs follow-up, and a booking needs a separate attendance record.

For a hospital or clinic owner, the practical question is where people are reaching the next step and where the process needs attention. Agree on the count definitions with your marketing and reception teams before comparing results or changing spend. Then use the same definitions every month.

How to count hospital enquiries versus appointments

Use a stable enquiry ID to connect the original request with follow-up and outcomes. These definitions provide a starting point for your team:

Enquiry and appointment stage definitions
StageWhat your team can verify
Captured enquiryA usable request exists in the intake record, with its received time and source. Mark spam, duplicates and tests separately.
Successfully contactedStaff had a two-way conversation with the person. A dial attempt, unanswered call or sent message remains an attempt.
Qualified enquiryStaff confirmed the administrative fit using agreed criteria, such as the requested service, location and intended next step. Record the reason.
Appointment bookedThe scheduling record confirms a date and time. An appointment request remains pending until confirmed.
Appointment attendedThe hospital’s attendance record confirms the visit. A booking alone cannot establish attendance.

For this funnel, count each enquiry ID once at each stage it reaches. Keep appointment totals separate if an enquiry leads to several bookings. Explain whether a report counts enquiry records, people or appointments; those totals can differ.

Record notification delivery separately too. An enquiry can be saved while its email notification fails. Equally, an analytics event can appear without a confirmed intake record. Keep capture status, email confirmation and human qualification as separate fields.

Use the same group and follow-up window

A monthly intake total and a monthly appointment total describe different activity. Some appointments this month may come from earlier enquiries. Some recent enquiries may still be waiting for a response or an available appointment.

To measure progression, start with a cohort: the valid enquiries received during one defined period. Track those same IDs through later stages until a stated review date. Use the same timezone and follow-up window when comparing cohorts. Leave recent outcomes pending rather than treating them as failures.

For that cohort:

  • Contact rate: enquiry IDs successfully contacted ÷ valid captured enquiry IDs.
  • Qualification rate: enquiry IDs staff marked qualified ÷ valid captured enquiry IDs.
  • Booking rate: enquiry IDs with a confirmed appointment ÷ valid captured enquiry IDs.
  • Attendance rate: eligible booked enquiry IDs with confirmed attendance ÷ eligible booked enquiry IDs. Define eligibility as the agreed appointment's scheduled date having passed by the review date.

Show the counts beside each percentage. Use the same eligible IDs in an attendance rate's numerator and denominator, and state which appointment is tracked when an enquiry has several bookings. State the treatment of cancellations, rescheduling and duplicates. If you calculate qualification among contacted enquiries instead, label that denominator explicitly. Keep unavailable outcomes marked unknown. If eligible attendance outcomes remain unknown, show that count beside the rate and label the rate provisional. When the denominator is zero, show the rate as unavailable.

What Totem’s published cases teach

The Sobti Nursing Home case study reports 306 Google Ads tracked conversion actions in July 2026. Its measurement note identifies these as advertising or analytics actions, without presenting them as confirmed patients or appointments. The engagement also examined geography and serviceability when assessing enquiry quality. A campaign report and an operational outcome record answer different questions.

The Virk Fertility case study reports 2,469 profile visits and zero recorded bio-link taps in July 2026. That pointed to a gap between discovery and the enquiry path. The published evidence does not establish a verified three-month appointment or patient total. It supports examining the next step, without inventing an outcome beyond the available records.

Give reception a clear response process

Make the handover usable for the people handling enquiries:

  1. Check receipt. Confirm that a usable enquiry record reached the intake system. Review failed notifications separately.
  2. Assign an owner. Give each enquiry a responsible person and a next-action time. Cover the handover when that person is unavailable.
  3. Agree response hours. Set a process your team can support, including enquiries received outside working hours. Record the first response time.
  4. Record contact attempts. Keep the attempt time and outcome, including unanswered or incorrect numbers. Separate these from successful contact.
  5. Confirm administrative fit. Use the agreed qualification criteria and record the next step. Keep clinical assessment within the hospital’s normal process.
  6. Confirm the booking. Link the scheduling reference to the enquiry ID. Update cancellations and rescheduling without deleting the history.
  7. Close the attendance record. Use the hospital’s recorded outcome rather than an advertising-platform estimate.

Keep contact details in the operational record. A management summary needs counts and reasons, without reproducing personal enquiry details.

Review the gap before choosing the next action

Review a settled cohort with the marketing and reception teams. Missing intake records suggest a capture problem. Recorded enquiries without contact attempts suggest a handover problem. Contacted enquiries awaiting booking need a recorded reason before you decide what to change. These are questions to investigate, not automatic explanations for performance.

A useful report connects campaign activity with verified steps and keeps unknown outcomes visible.
